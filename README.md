## 🏛️ Executive Overview

Modern global infrastructure is overwhelmingly designed around the anatomy, biomechanics, and anthropometric dimensions of humans. In a multi-species society, applying a single human-centered design standard creates catastrophic limitations in accessibility, safety, comfort, productivity, and emergency mobility.

The **Furgonomic Engineering Standard (FES)** establishes a coherent, systematic engineering methodology to govern the design of shared civilization. Rather than designing independent infrastructure for every individual species, furgonomic engineering treats species-specific anatomical features as engineering variables scaled against a controlled baseline.

### The Four Levels of Design
FES architecture distinguishes between four distinct levels of structural deployment:
1. **Universal Infrastructure:** Safely and effectively serves most species without modification.
2. **Adjustable Infrastructure:** Accommodates biological variation through mechanical adjustments (seating, work surfaces, controls, restraints, and PPE).
3. **Species-Adapted Infrastructure:** Requires specific modifications due to distinct anatomical characteristics (wings, tails, horns, hooves, dense fur).
4. **Specialized Infrastructure:** Necessary for species whose morphology falls substantially outside the standard anthropomorphic body plan.

## 📐 Core Architecture: SARM-01

The foundation of the FES framework is **SARM-01 (Standard Anthropomorphic Reference Morphology)**. 

SARM-01 is a generalized, baseline human control morphology against which all non-humanoid biological deviations are calculated. By treating non-human biology as mathematical modifiers to the SARM-01 baseline, engineers can calculate clearance, reach, and safety envelopes systematically.
